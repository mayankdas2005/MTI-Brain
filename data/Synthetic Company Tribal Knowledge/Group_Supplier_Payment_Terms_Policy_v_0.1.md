## **Purpose and Scope** 

This policy establishes the principles, definitions, decision criteria, and governance the Company applies when setting and changing supplier payment terms. It standardizes how payment-term tiers are assigned, how the working-capital and cash-flow consequences of term changes are measured, and how proposed changes are reviewed, approved, and communicated to suppliers. 

The policy applies to all trade-payable relationships with external suppliers of goods and services across every legal entity, business unit, and geography the Company consolidates. It governs the standard payment-term framework, the renegotiation of existing terms (including the migration of open invoices from one tier to another), and the reporting that supports those decisions. It does not cover employee payroll, intercompany settlements, tax remittances, lease obligations, or debt service, each of which is governed by its own policy. 

## **Objectives** 

- Apply consistent, defensible payment terms to comparable suppliers. 

- Optimize working capital and Days Payable Outstanding (DPO) without impairing supply continuity, supplier viability, or contractual and regulatory compliance. 

- Ensure every term change is supported by a quantified cash-flow and working-capital assessment before it is approved or communicated. 

- Protect smaller and strategically critical suppliers from terms that would breach regulation or jeopardize the relationship. 

## **Definitions** 

The following terms have consistent meaning throughout this policy and across all reporting and analytics derived from it. 

|**Term**|**Definition**|
|---|---|
|Payment term|The contractual number of calendar days, measured from the invoice<br>reference date, within which an undisputed supplier invoice must be<br>settled.|
|Net-30 / Net-60 / Net-90|Standard payment-term tiers requiring settlement within 30, 60, or 90<br>calendar days of the invoice reference date, respectively.|
|Invoice reference date|The date from which the payment term is counted. Default is the invoice<br>receipt date unless the contract specifies invoice date or goods-receipt<br>date.|
|Open invoice|An approved, undisputed supplier invoice that has been recorded as a<br>payable but not yet settled as at the measurement date.|
|Open payables balance|The total value of open invoices for a supplier or supplier group as at the<br>measurement date.|
|Days Payable<br>Outstanding (DPO)|Average number of days the Company takes to pay its suppliers,<br>calculated per_Working-Capital and DPO Methodology_section|
|Working-capital benefit|The one-time cash released, plus the recurring financing benefit, from<br>deferring payment by extending a payment term. See section on_Working-_<br>_Capital and DPO Methodology_.|

## **Standard Payment-Term Framework** 

Unless a binding contract specifies otherwise, suppliers are assigned to a standard payment-term tier. The default tier is determined by supplier category and the assignment rules in Supplier classification and term assignment section. Non-standard terms require approval under the section on _Governance, Roles and Approvals_ . 

|**Tier**|**Standard application**|**Primary purpose**|
|---|---|---|
|Net-30|Default tier for most trade suppliers, and the<br>mandatory tier for protected suppliers under the<br>section on_Protected Suppliers and Rollout_<br>_Exclusions_.|•<br>Baseline terms definition<br>•<br>Regulatory and small-<br>supplier compliance.|
|Net-60|Mid-tier suppliers where contracts or market<br>norms support extended terms.|•<br>Balanced working-capital<br>position.|
|Net-90|Large, financially resilient suppliers able to absorb<br>extended terms without distress.|•<br>Working-capital<br>optimization<br>•<br>DPO improvement.|



## **Supplier Classification and Term Assignment** 

Every supplier is classified so that comparable suppliers receive comparable terms. Classification drives the default tier and determines eligibility for term changes. 

## **Classification dimensions** 

- **Annual spend band:** The Company's trailing-twelve-month spend with the supplier. 

- Supplier size: enterprise size of the supplier, used to identify small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) for protection under _Protected Suppliers and Rollout exclusions_ . 

- **Criticality:** strategic, sole-source, or standard, per sub-section on _Strategic and sole-source designation_ . 

- **Commercial features:** presence of early-payment discounts, contractual term locks, or minimum-payment-term clauses.

## **Strategic and sole-source designation** 

A supplier is designated strategic where loss or disruption would materially affect operations, revenue, safety, or compliance, or where the supplier provides a sole or single source for a critical input. Designation is proposed by Procurement, reviewed by the relevant business unit, and approved by the CFO. The current strategic and sole-source register is maintained by Procurement and is authoritative for all term decisions. 

## **Working-Capital and DPO Methodology** 

This section defines the standard calculations used to quantify the effect of extending payment terms. All reporting and analytics use these definitions so that results are reproducible and comparable. 

## **Days Payable Outstanding** 

DPO = (Average open payables balance ÷ Total credit purchases for the period) × Number of days in the period. 

DPO is measured at total-company level and may be measured for any supplier subset (for example, the Net-30 population) using the same formula restricted to that subset. 

## **Working-capital benefit of extending a term** 

Extending a payment term defers cash outflow. The benefit has two components, measured per supplier and aggregated across the affected population. 

|**Component**|**Definition**|**Formula**|
|---|---|---|
|One-time cash release|Cash retained by deferring the open<br>payables that fall within the extension<br>window.|Open payables eligible to shift ×<br>(Extension days ÷ Original term<br>days), capped at the eligible open<br>balance.|

**Worked basis for a Net-30 to Net-90 extension** 

**Extension days:** 90 - 30 = 60 additional days of deferral per affected invoice. 

**One-time cash release:** The value of open Net-30 invoices that shift, deferred by up to 60 days or roughly 60 days of credit purchases with the affected suppliers. 

**Recurring benefit:** Released cash × 9.0% WACC, pro-rated for the 60-day deferral. 

**DPO impact** : Increase in blended DPO once the affected Net-30 spend is paid over 90 days instead of 30 and reported in days at both subset and total-company level. 

## **Payment-Term Renegotiation Framework** 

Migrating suppliers between tier, say, extending open Net-30 supplier invoices to Net-90. It is permitted only through the structured process below. 

## **Eligibility screen** 

A Net-30 supplier is eligible for extension only if it passes every test: 

- It is not a protected supplier under section on _Protected Suppliers and Rollout Exclusions_ . 

- It is not bound by a contractual minimum term shorter than the proposed term. 

- It is assessed as financially resilient enough to absorb the extension without distress. 

- Its open payables exceed the materiality threshold set by Treasury. 

## **Cash-flow impact ranking** 

Eligible suppliers are ranked so the largest opportunities are addressed first. The ranking metric is the supplier-level cash-flow impact: 

Cash-flow impact = Open Net-30 payables eligible to shift × (Extension days ÷ 365) × WACC, plus the one-time cash release. 

Suppliers with the largest eligible open balances and the longest extension windows produce the largest cash-flow impact and rank highest. Reporting that asks _which Net-30 suppliers create the largest cash-flow impact if renegotiated to Net-90_ returns this ranked list, restricted to eligible suppliers under section on _Eligibility_ .

## **Protected Suppliers and Rollout Exclusions** 

Before any supplier notices are issued, the proposed population must be screened against the exclusion criteria below. Any supplier meeting one or more criteria is excluded from the extension rollout and, where applicable, locked at Net-30. Exclusions are documented in the rollout file and retained for audit. 

- **E1** Regulated SMEs. Suppliers qualifying as small or medium-sized enterprises where promptpayment regulation, statutory codes, or signed payment commitments mandate terms at or below Net-30. 

- **E2** Early-payment-discount suppliers where the discount yield exceeds the cost of capital. Extending payment would forfeit a discount worth more than the financing benefit and these remain on their current terms. 

- **E3** Strategic and sole-source suppliers. Critical or single-source suppliers whose relationship or continuity risk outweighs the working-capital benefit, unless individually approved under section on _Approval thresholds_ . 

- **E4** Contractually locked suppliers. Suppliers bound by a minimum payment term or a clause prohibiting unilateral term changes for the contract duration. 

- **E5** Financially distressed suppliers. Suppliers on the watch list or showing distress indicators, where extended terms could trigger failure and supply disruption. 

- **E6** Immaterial suppliers. Suppliers whose eligible open payables fall below the materiality threshold, where the administrative cost outweighs the benefit. 

- **E7** Disputed or on-hold accounts. Suppliers with open disputes, quality holds, or reconciliation issues, excluded until resolved.

## **Reporting and the CFO Term-Change Dashboard** 

Every proposed term migration is summarized on a standard one-page CFO dashboard before approval. The dashboard provides a consistent decision view and is the authoritative format for executive review. 

## **Required dashboard fields** 

|**Field**|**Definition**|**Source section**|
|---|---|---|
|Total cash benefit|Aggregate working-capital benefit (one-<br>time release plus recurring financing<br>benefit) of the proposed change.|Working-capital benefit of<br>extending a term|
|Impacted supplier count|Number of eligible suppliers in scope after<br>exclusions.|Protected Suppliers and<br>Rollout Exclusions, and<br>Eligibility screen|
|DPO impact|Change in blended DPO, in days, at subset<br>and total-company level.|Days Payable Outstanding|
|Top contributors|Ranked suppliers by cash-flow impact.|Cash-flow impact ranking|
|Excluded suppliers|Count and reason codes for suppliers<br>removed from scope.|Protected Suppliers and<br>Rollout Exclusions|
|Recommendation|Proceed / proceed with exclusions / defer,<br>with rationale and required approval level.|Recommendation Logic|



## **Recommendation logic** 

The dashboard recommendation is derived consistently: 

- Proceed where the total cash benefit clears the approval threshold, exclusions are applied, and no strategic supplier is adversely affected. 

- Proceed with exclusions where the benefit is material but specific suppliers must be held back under section _Protected Suppliers and Rollout Exclusions_ . 

- Defer where the benefit is immaterial, regulatory risk is unresolved, or supplier-distress risk is elevated.

## **Supplier Notification Protocol** 

Supplier notices may be issued only after the population has been screened for exclusions (Protected Suppliers and Rollout Exclusions section), the change has been approved ( _Approval thresholds section_ ), and Legal has confirmed there are no contractual or regulatory barriers. Notices are issued by Procurement, follow the approved template, provide the required notice period, and identify a contact for queries. Suppliers raising hardship or contractual objections are escalated for case-bycase review before the change takes effect. 

## **Compliance and Regulatory Considerations** 

Term changes must comply with all applicable prompt-payment legislation, statutory or voluntary payment codes, and contractual commitments in every jurisdiction in which the Company operates. Where local regulation mandates terms more favourable to the supplier than this policy, the local regulation prevails. Legal maintains the register of applicable requirements, and Internal Audit tests adherence.
