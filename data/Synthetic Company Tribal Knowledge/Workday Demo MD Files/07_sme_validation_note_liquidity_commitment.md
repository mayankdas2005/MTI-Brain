# SME Validation Note — Liquidity Commitment

## Validation purpose

Validate whether the CFO's March liquidity statement to the Audit & Risk Committee should be treated as active institutional memory for the May treasury health briefing.

## Validated fact

The CFO made an informal but active management commitment to the Audit & Risk Committee to maintain at least **USD 250M of total liquidity** through the current planning window.

This commitment is:

- active for the current May–August forecast window;
- relevant to CFO briefing recommendations;
- not incorporated into the formal Group Treasury Policy;
- not a replacement for the formal USD 200M hard minimum threshold;
- not a replacement for the USD 300M target operating buffer;
- material when forecast liquidity falls below USD 250M, even if it remains above USD 200M.

## Evidence reviewed

| Evidence | Reviewed by | Validation result |
|---|---|---|
| March Audit & Risk Committee prebrief excerpt | Michael Grant | Confirms CFO statement and Committee context |
| CFO monthly finance / treasury notes from April | Jonathan Reed | Confirms CFO reminded Treasury to assess Board-readiness |
| May Teams transcript excerpt | Lina Ortiz | Confirms CFO wants action if trough remains below USD 250M |
| Treasury Committee notes from 6 May | Jonathan Reed | Confirms forecast low point and Amber status |

## SME conclusion

The USD 250M liquidity level should be treated as a governed management commitment for briefing purposes. It should not be represented as a formal Board-approved policy limit.

When preparing the CFO liquidity briefing, the correct interpretation is:

1. The forecast remains above the formal USD 200M hard floor.
2. The forecast is Amber under the action framework because it falls below the target operating buffer and below the formal Green posture.
3. The forecast creates a distinct Board-commitment risk because the low point falls below the CFO's USD 250M commitment.
4. The recommended action should be more specific than routine monitoring: provide mitigation options, quantify sensitivity, and prepare CFO messaging before the briefing.

## Recommended wording for internal use

> The forecast does not create a formal liquidity breach. However, the current forecast trough is inconsistent with the active management commitment communicated to the Audit & Risk Committee. Treasury should treat this as requiring CFO review and mitigation or messaging before the briefing.
