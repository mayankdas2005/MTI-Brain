# Treasury Committee Liquidity Review — 6 May 2026

## Summary

The Committee reviewed the May forecast refresh and confirmed that the Group remains above the formal hard minimum liquidity threshold throughout the planning window. The current position remains above the target operating buffer. However, the rolling forecast shows a sustained dip below the target operating buffer beginning in Week 3 and a forecast low point in Week 8.

The Committee agreed the position is not a Red liquidity breach under policy because no forecast week falls below **USD 200M**. The Committee also agreed that the position requires CFO-level attention because the forecast enters an Amber monitoring range and because the Week 8 forecast low point is close to the level discussed with the Audit & Risk Committee.

## Key liquidity drivers

1. **Inventory receipts.** Seasonal summer inventory receipts create an expected cash outflow spike in Weeks 4–6.
2. **Payroll calendar.** Two payroll cycles fall inside the same short-term window as supplier prepayments.
3. **Tax and duty payments.** Indirect tax and import duty payments are scheduled ahead of the revenue recovery point.
4. **Receivables recovery.** The forecast assumes two large receivables clear in Week 9. The Committee noted timing slippage would extend the trough period.
5. **Facility availability.** The committed facility remains available, but drawing it solely for buffer optics should be treated as a CFO decision.

## Committee discussion

Jonathan stated that, from a strict policy point of view, the forecast remains above the hard minimum threshold. Lina confirmed that the formal status should be Amber rather than Red. Priya noted that the CFO will likely ask whether the Week 8 trough has any Board-readout implications because it sits below the level management discussed informally with the Audit & Risk Committee.

Elena recommended that the CFO briefing separate three concepts:

- the **hard floor** of USD 200M;
- the **target operating buffer** of USD 300M;
- the **management comfort level** discussed with the Audit & Risk Committee, currently understood by Treasury leadership as USD 250M through the current planning window.

## Decisions

- The May CFO briefing must include the full 13-week liquidity path, not only the current balance.
- The Week 8 low point should be called out as the forecast trough.
- The Committee does not recommend immediate emergency escalation.
- Treasury should prepare mitigating options before the CFO briefing because the forecast sits below the management comfort level for several weeks.
